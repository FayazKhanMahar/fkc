Study in Italy

A step-by-step guide for Pakistani students applying to Italian universities — covering Bachelor's, Master's and PhD, plus the fully-funded regional scholarship.

Why Italy

One of Europe's most affordable funded destinations

Public universities

Italy's public universities are globally respected, with low tuition and many programmes taught fully in English.

Often free to apply

Most applications are online, and many universities charge no application fee — you can apply to several at once.

Funded scholarships

Regional government scholarships can cover tuition, accommodation, meals, and a living stipend (details below).

Admissions for international students generally open around January to March/April, but every university sets its own dates — always confirm on the official university website.

Get ready

Documents required for admission

The core set most Italian universities ask for when you apply. Requirements can vary by university and programme.

Educational documents

Degrees, transcripts (DMCs), and certificates from your previous studies.

Motivation letter

A statement explaining why you want the programme and why you're a good fit.

Passport copy

A valid passport (check it has enough validity for your intended stay).

Language certificate

English (IELTS/TOEFL) or Italian (CILS/CELI), as required by the programme.

Recommendation letters

Usually from professors or employers who can speak to your ability.

CV

An up-to-date academic/professional CV. Job certificates are optional but help.

For the embassy

Student visa documents

Pakistani students apply for a Type D long-stay student visa (for programmes longer than 90 days). A typical document checklist includes:

Visa application form

Passport & photocopies

Admission / acceptance letter

Declaration of Value (DOV)

Educational documents + translation

IELTS (embassy generally requires 6.0+)

Proof of funds (recent bank statement)

Sponsor affidavit & documents

Health insurance (min. €30,000 cover)

Accommodation proof

Family Registration Certificate (NADRA)

Photographs & CV

The consulate/embassy provides the official, up-to-date checklist — requirements and amounts can change, so always confirm before you submit.

Funding

The LazioDiSCo scholarship (Rome & Lazio region)

Most Italian regions offer their own government-funded "right to study" scholarship. In the Lazio region (Rome and surrounding cities), it's LazioDiSCo — open to all nationalities, for Bachelor's, Master's and PhD students at public universities.

What it can cover

  • 100% tuition coverage
  • Free university accommodation, or housing support
  • A monthly living stipend
  • Free meals at university dining facilities
  • Health insurance contribution for international students

How much & when

The cash amount is income-based (lower family income = larger grant). For recent years it has ranged roughly from €3,300 up to about €7,600 per year, paid in installments — in addition to tuition, housing and meal benefits.

It's typically announced around June–July with a summer deadline. Scholarship and visa are two separate processes — don't mix them.

Important: scholarship amounts, income thresholds, and deadlines change every year. The figures above are recent estimates — always confirm the current call on the official LazioDiSCo website, or ask us for the latest.
